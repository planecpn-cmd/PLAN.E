## 2. If you are in immediate danger

1. **Get to safety first.** Move away from the hazard before doing anything else
2. **Tell your guide.** They are trained, present, and have the local knowledge
3. **Call emergency services** — 100 for police, 102 for ambulance
4. **Call the Plan E emergency line** once immediate danger has passed, or ask someone
   to call on your behalf

Do not delay calling emergency services in order to contact Plan E first. We coordinate;
we do not rescue.

## 3. Who does what

### The host and guide — first response
- Provide immediate first aid
- Assess and decide: treat on site, descend, or evacuate
- Contact emergency and rescue services
- Contact Plan E
- Contact the participant's emergency contact
- Keep the rest of the group safe and informed
- Continue to accompany or arrange care for the affected person until handed to medical
  services

### Plan E — coordination and support
- Answer the emergency line 24/7 and act as a single point of contact
- Hold your emergency contact and, where provided, insurance details, and pass them to
  rescue and medical services
- Liaise between the host, your family, your insurer and the authorities
- Assist with logistics: onward travel, accommodation, replacement arrangements
- Assist with embassy or consular contact for foreign nationals
- Log and investigate the incident afterwards

**Plan E does not conduct rescues, provide medical care, or pay for evacuation.**

### You — before it happens
- Provide a working emergency contact at booking
- Hold appropriate insurance and carry the policy number and its 24-hour line
- Tell your guide about medical conditions before starting
- Carry ID and, for foreign nationals, a copy of your passport and visa

## 4. Medical emergencies

The guide provides first aid and decides on evacuation. For altitude illness the
treatment is **immediate descent** — this is not optional and not negotiable.

Where helicopter evacuation is needed, the guide or Plan E contacts a rescue operator.
Flights depend on **weather, daylight and availability** and may be delayed or
impossible.

**Cost.** Helicopter evacuation in Nepal typically costs several thousand US dollars and
is payable by you or your insurer. Operators usually require a payment guarantee before
launching — this is why your insurer's 24-hour line matters, and why you must carry the
number. Plan E will help make the call and provide the operator with booking details, but
**cannot guarantee or fund the flight**.

## 5. Missing person

If a participant is missing:

1. The guide initiates an immediate search of the last known area and accounts for the
   rest of the group
2. Police and, in mountain areas, the relevant rescue authority are notified
3. Plan E is notified immediately and contacts the emergency contact
4. For foreign nationals, Plan E assists with embassy notification
5. Plan E provides authorities with the booking record, itinerary, participant details
   and last known plan

## 6. Natural disaster and civil disruption

Earthquake, landslide, flood, avalanche, epidemic, strike, bandh or civil unrest:

- The host's first duty is to move the group to safety and account for everyone
- Plan E attempts to contact all travellers with active bookings in the affected area
- We coordinate with authorities and, where possible, with your embassy
- We assist with alternative arrangements and onward travel

Refund and credit treatment is under the Cancellation Policy, section 4. Financial
questions wait until everyone is safe.

## 7. Death of a participant

Handled with the involvement of Nepal Police, the relevant embassy where applicable, and
the family. Plan E provides all booking records to the authorities and to the family,
assists with consular liaison and repatriation logistics, and conducts a full internal
investigation.

Any refund is issued to the estate without deduction and without requiring a claim.

## 8. Incident reporting and investigation

Hosts report incidents to Plan E within **24 hours**, immediately where serious.

Every report is investigated: accounts from all parties, equipment and licensing records,
conditions, and the host's compliance with the Safety and Risk Policy. Findings may lead
to host suspension or removal, listing changes, or referral to authorities.

Affected travellers receive the findings relevant to them in writing.

Records are kept **7 years**.

## 9. Contacting family

If we cannot reach you and there is reasonable concern for your safety, we may contact
your emergency contact and share the information necessary to locate and assist you.
This is permitted under the Individual Privacy Act, 2075 where necessary to protect a
person's life.

## 10. After an emergency

We will contact you to check on you, explain what happened and why, tell you what we
found, provide documentation for insurance claims, and handle any refund without
requiring you to argue for it.

Trauma support is not something we can provide directly, but we will help you find it.
