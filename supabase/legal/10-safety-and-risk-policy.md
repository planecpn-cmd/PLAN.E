# Safety and Risk Policy

**Plan E** · Effective: [EFFECTIVE DATE]

Plan E lists experiences that carry real risk — trekking at altitude, climbing, rafting,
paragliding, remote travel. This policy sets out what we do, what hosts must do, and
what you must do.

---

## 1. Honest starting point

Adventure activities in Nepal cannot be made risk-free. Terrain is remote, weather
changes fast, altitude affects people unpredictably, medical facilities outside the
Kathmandu Valley are limited, and evacuation can take hours or days.

We can reduce risk through host standards and clear information. We cannot remove it.
Anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.

## 2. What Plan E does

- **Host verification** — identity, government ID, and the licences the activity
  requires under Nepali law before a listing goes live
- **Licence checks** for regulated activities: Nepal Tourism Board registration,
  trekking agency licensing, rafting and paragliding operator permissions
- **Listing review** — difficulty ratings, minimum age, fitness requirements and
  required equipment must be stated before publication
- **Risk disclosure** — high-risk activities carry a risk acknowledgment step at booking
- **Incident recording** — every reported incident is logged, investigated and reviewed
- **Removal** — hosts who breach safety standards are suspended or removed

**What we do not do:** we are not present on experiences, we do not supervise guides in
the field, and we do not audit every trip. Verification at listing time is not a
guarantee of conduct on the day.

## 3. What hosts must do

Mandatory for every host on the platform.

### Licensing and staff
- Hold all licences and permits the activity requires and keep them current
- Use guides trained and certified for the activity, at appropriate guide-to-client
  ratios
- Ensure guides hold current wilderness or remote first aid certification where the
  activity takes place beyond quick medical access

### Equipment
- Provide safety equipment in serviceable condition, inspected before each use
- Retire equipment at or before manufacturer life limits
- Carry a first aid kit appropriate to the activity, group size and remoteness

### Communication
- Carry means of communication that works where you operate — mobile where covered,
  satellite phone or radio where not
- Hold a written emergency plan naming the nearest medical facility, evacuation route,
  and helicopter landing points where relevant

### Briefing
- Brief every group before starting: hazards, procedures, emergency signals, group rules
- Confirm every participant meets the stated fitness and age requirements
- Collect and carry emergency contact details

### Judgment
- Turn back, reroute or cancel when conditions require it. **A safety cancellation is
  never penalised by Plan E** — a host who cancels for safety is treated as having acted
  correctly, and the traveller receives a full refund
- Never proceed under commercial pressure from clients

### Porter welfare
Where porters are used, hosts must provide adequate clothing, footwear and shelter,
observe load limits, provide insurance, and arrange care and evacuation for a porter who
becomes ill or injured. Porter welfare failures are treated as safety breaches.

## 4. What you must do

- **Disclose honestly** — heart conditions, respiratory conditions, diabetes, epilepsy,
  pregnancy, recent surgery, mobility limitations, allergies, and current medication.
  Guides make decisions based on what they know about you
- **Be realistic about fitness.** A "moderate" multi-day trek is harder than most people
  expect. Overestimating yourself endangers you and your group
- **Bring the required equipment.** The list is a requirement
- **Follow instructions.** On safety matters the guide's decision is final
- **Speak up early** — headache, nausea, breathlessness, dizziness, or any doubt. Early
  reporting is the single most effective safety behaviour available to you
- **Do not pressure a guide** to continue when they have decided to turn back

## 5. Altitude

For anything above 3,000 m:

- Altitude sickness can affect anyone regardless of age or fitness, and previous success
  at altitude does not confer immunity
- Know the symptoms: headache, nausea, fatigue, dizziness, loss of appetite, disturbed
  sleep. Worsening symptoms, confusion, loss of coordination or breathlessness at rest
  are emergencies requiring **immediate descent**
- Follow the itinerary's acclimatisation schedule. It is not padding
- Report symptoms immediately. Do not conceal them to avoid delaying the group
- **Descent is the treatment.** A guide directing descent is not negotiable

## 6. Insurance

**Plan E does not provide insurance, and does not arrange it.**

Travel insurance is **strongly recommended** for every experience and **required** where
a listing says so. For trekking and climbing it should cover:

- Emergency medical treatment in Nepal
- **Helicopter evacuation** — check the altitude limit on the policy. Many standard
  policies cap below the altitude of common treks
- Trip cancellation and interruption
- The specific activity — many policies exclude mountaineering, climbing or paragliding
  unless declared

**Read the exclusions.** An evacuation from a high camp can cost several thousand US
dollars and is payable by you or your insurer, not by Plan E or the host.

State your insurance details at booking where asked, and carry the policy number and
emergency line with you.

## 7. Health and medical

Consult a doctor before any strenuous or high-altitude experience, particularly with any
cardiovascular, respiratory or metabolic condition or during pregnancy.

Carry your own medication in sufficient quantity, in original packaging, plus a copy of
the prescription. Do not assume it is available in Nepal.

Guides carry first aid, not medical qualifications. They provide first response and
arrange evacuation. They are not a substitute for medical care.

## 8. Incidents

Every incident — injury, illness requiring evacuation, near miss, equipment failure,
lost participant — must be reported by the host to Plan E within **24 hours**, or
immediately where serious.

We investigate every report, may suspend the host during investigation, and act on the
findings. Records are kept for 7 years.

If you are involved in or witness an incident, report it at [SUPPORT EMAIL] or
[EMERGENCY HOTLINE] even if the host has already done so. Independent accounts matter.

## 9. Your right to stop

**You may withdraw from any experience at any time if you feel unsafe.** Tell the guide.
Refund entitlement depends on the circumstances, but no refund question should ever
influence a safety decision.

If a host pressures you to continue when you have said you feel unsafe, report it. It is
a serious breach.

## 10. Reporting a safety concern

Immediate danger: **[EMERGENCY HOTLINE]**, and Nepal Police **100**
Non-urgent: [SUPPORT EMAIL], or report the listing in the app

Safety reports are prioritised above all other complaints and are never dismissed for
being informal or incomplete.
